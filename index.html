01 · THE BASICS

So, what exactly is PCOS?

PCOS stands for polycystic ovary syndrome. It is a complex hormonal and metabolic condition that can affect how the ovaries function.

It may be associated with irregular ovulation, higher levels of androgens (hormones such as testosterone), and changes in the appearance of the ovaries on ultrasound.

Despite its name, a person does not need to have ovarian cysts on an ultrasound to be diagnosed with PCOS.

✦

Remember: having one symptom does not automatically mean you have PCOS.

02 · UNDERSTANDING SYMPTOMS

What changes might someone notice?

Symptoms can vary in type and severity. Some may also have causes unrelated to PCOS.

◷

Menstrual changes

Periods that are irregular, infrequent or absent can sometimes be associated with irregular ovulation.

✧

Skin changes

Persistent acne or oily skin may occur, including when androgen levels are elevated.

❋

Hair changes

Some people notice increased facial or body hair, or thinning hair on the scalp.

↗

Metabolic changes

PCOS can be associated with insulin resistance and a higher risk of certain metabolic conditions.

Recognizing a change is a first step.

A symptom list alone cannot confirm or exclude PCOS.

03 · GETTING ANSWERS

How is PCOS diagnosed?

There is no single test that confirms PCOS in every person. A qualified healthcare professional considers the overall picture.

01

Health and menstrual history

A clinician may ask about periods, symptoms, medicines, medical history and how long changes have been present.

02

Examination, when appropriate

Depending on age, symptoms and circumstances, a clinician may recommend a physical examination with your consent.

03

Blood tests, if needed

Tests may assess hormones and check for other explanations. Glucose and cholesterol testing may also be considered.

04

Ultrasound, in selected cases

Ultrasound may be useful for some people, but it is not required in every case. Diagnostic criteria differ for adolescents and adults.

i

In adults, diagnosis commonly involves assessing combinations of ovulatory dysfunction, clinical or biochemical evidence of excess androgens, and polycystic ovarian appearance, after excluding other causes. An ultrasound is not always necessary. Adolescents require different, age-appropriate criteria.

04 · SUPPORT & MANAGEMENT

Care that fits the person.

PCOS management depends on symptoms, health needs and personal goals. There is no single approach that suits everyone.

♡

Everyday wellbeing

Regular physical activity, balanced nutrition, sufficient sleep and sustainable habits can support overall health.

LISTEN TO YOUR BODY

When should you seek medical help?

Consider arranging a healthcare appointment for persistent menstrual changes, new or worsening symptoms, or concerns about your health.

Seek urgent medical care for severe or sudden pelvic or abdominal pain, fainting, or heavy bleeding accompanied by dizziness, weakness or feeling seriously unwell.

STILL CURIOUS?

Frequently asked questions.

Is PCOS the same as PCOD?

The terms are sometimes used interchangeably in everyday conversation, but terminology can vary. PCOS is the widely used medical term for polycystic ovary syndrome. Ask your clinician what a specific diagnosis means for you.

Can PCOS be cured?

There is currently no single cure, but symptoms and associated health risks can often be managed with individualized care.

Can someone have PCOS without obvious symptoms?

Symptoms vary, and some signs may be subtle. A diagnosis requires appropriate clinical assessment, not just a checklist.

Can this page tell me whether I have PCOS?

No. This page provides general education only. A qualified healthcare professional can evaluate your history, symptoms and any tests that may be appropriate.

PCOS, in short

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal condition. Many people live with it for years before it is recognised, because the signs look different from person to person.

Only a doctor can confirm it, usually with a history, blood tests and sometimes an ultrasound.

Signs that are often discussed

  • Periods that are infrequent, very irregular or absent
  • Acne or oily skin that keeps coming back
  • Thicker hair on the face, chest or belly
  • Thinning hair on the scalp
  • Weight gain, especially around the waist
  • Darker, velvety skin on the neck or underarms

Other conditions, such as thyroid problems, can look similar.

When to see a doctor

  1. Soon (within a few weeks): periods missing for 3 months or more, cycles that keep changing, new acne or hair changes that bother you.
  2. Which doctor: a gynaecologist is a good start. An endocrinologist or dermatologist can help with hormone and skin concerns.
  3. Right away: very heavy bleeding, severe pelvic pain, fainting, or any chance of pregnancy with pain or bleeding. Go to the nearest emergency department.

Important: SUGAMYA is an information tool. It does not diagnose, prescribe or replace a qualified doctor. Do not delay seeking care because of what you read here.
